## Things that look broken
1. **Data loader cuts off backtest history (quant/data.py, `download`).** The loader drops any date on which fewer than half of *today's* 2,257 stocks have a price. Since the universe became all NSE stocks, that removes every date before about March 2017, because many of today's stocks were not listed then. The 10-year test becomes 9.7 years with a cold start, and nothing warns you. A one-line fix tested in scratch compares each day with nearby days instead of with today's stock count, and it reproduces the previous results exactly. This is a code fix, not a rule change, but it is inside `quant/`, so it needs your OK. Live daily runs are not affected (recent dates have more than 90% coverage).
2. **The month-end ETF trade breaks the 25% size rule.** `TOM weight = 0.5` puts about 50% of equity into MID150BEES, and its "stop" is set at half the price (effectively no stop). In the 10-year backtest, all 84 of these trades were sized at 47–51% of equity. 8 of them lost more than 1% of equity (Rs 1,000 on Rs 1 lakh), and the worst lost about Rs 3,200 per lakh. The next entry is 29 Dec.
3. **BTST and swing can lose more than Rs 1,000.** Sizing aims at Rs 500 (BTST) and Rs 750 (swing), but BTST has no live stop. In the backtest, 33 of 1,267 BTST trades (2.6%) and 7 of 596 swing trades (1.2%) lost more than 1% of equity, with the worst BTST trade at about Rs 3,600 per lakh. These BTST losses were **not** gaps through the stop: only 10 BTST trades opened below the stop. They were falls during the next day, before the planned exit at the close. The swing losses were mostly gaps through the stop or avoid-signal exits.
4. Minor: swing exits are still labelled "20-day time stop" although they now exit after 5 sessions. The 27 Nov morning brief (`out/morning.txt`, made at 14:25 before the fast-churn change) still lists three positional buys. That brief will be replaced by the next run.

## Proposed improvements (for your approval; nothing changed)
**1. Cap the month-end ETF trade at 25% of equity** (`TOM weight` 0.5 → 0.25 in quant/config.py).
Evidence (10-year backtest, scratch run): 84 trades, typically sized at about 22% of equity (largest 25.6%, because of rounding). Losses above Rs 1,000 per lakh fall from 8 to 2, and the worst falls from about Rs 3,200 to Rs 1,700. Cost: 15.0% → 12.8% a year, Sharpe 1.47 → 1.38. Worst fall improves from −17.8% to −14.1%. This brings it within the 25% rule. Adding a 4% stop would also cap it at Rs 1,000, but I have not tested that.

**2. Make Rs 1,000 a hard limit on BTST with a stop-loss order on day 2** at the signal-day low (the stop already used for sizing).
Evidence (applied to the 1,267 backtest BTST trades): losses above Rs 1,000 per lakh fall from 33 to 1, and the worst falls from about Rs 3,600 to Rs 1,200 (one gap). Cost: BTST profit over 10 years falls from about Rs 78,000 to Rs 50,000 (−36%), and the win rate falls from 45.5% to 43.1%. This assumes the stop fills exactly at the stop price, so the real cost is a bit higher. An alternative with a similar cost is capping each BTST trade at 10% of equity: losses above Rs 1,000 fall from 33 to 6, the worst is about Rs 2,200, and profit is about Rs 50,000. Either way, a strict Rs 1,000 limit costs about a third of BTST profit. Your call.

All proposals stay within the 5-session (7-day) maximum hold.
